The Education Divide: Participation, Equity and Outcomes Across Australia

Across Australia, access to quality education is not always equal. Where do educational inequalities exist, how do outcomes differ between communities, and what do these patterns reveal about educational equity across Australia.

Education rates

Year 12 and secondary edication outcomes differ between states and territories.

A map of Completion

Proportion of Population who completed Year 12 or equivalent(%)

From 2011 to 2022, South Australia has the highest rates of complletion while Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and New South Wales have generally steady rates.


In 2022, South Australia had the highest Year 12 completion at 89%, followed by Victoria (83%) and Queensland (78%). Western Australia (76%), the ACT (74%) and New South Wales (71%) sit in the middle. Tasmania (53%) and the Northern Territory (42%) are well below the rest, and the Northern Territory rate is less than half that of South Australia.

How has Year 12 Completion changed & how do States compare?

South Australia has had the highest completion since 2016. Victoria climbed from sixth in 2009 to second by 2021, and Queensland has also increased since 2019, finishing third.

Some states have fallen back: the ACT dropped to fifth in 2021 and 2022. New South Wales has sat in the lower half since 2012, and Tasmania and the Northern Territory have continually had the lowest completion over the last decade. Ranks show order, not the size of the gap, so a state can fall in rank even while its completion rate rises.

Student Resources

Number Students per number of teaching and non-teaching staff.

Across all three sectors, the number of students per staff member rises as schools become more advantaged. Schools in the least advantaged group have the most staff for each student, and those in the most advantaged group have the fewest. Government schools have the most students per staff member at every level of advantage, rising from about 4 at the bottom of the ranking to about 10 at the top. Independent schools have the fewest, from about 3 to just over 5, with Catholic schools in between. The trend flattens or dips slightly for Catholic and independent schools at the very top of the ranking.

Does gender matter?

Females have higher Year 12 completion rates than Males across all states and territories.

The largest gap of 14.8% is in South Australia despite the highest overall completion rates. On the other hand, the Northern Territory has the lowest completion rates for Males and Females, both under 50% of the population completing Year 12.

Geographic Location also matters

Australian remoteness areas are classified by how far they are from services: major cities, regional areas and remote areas.

Students with Year 12 certification (2009-2022)

In 2022, Year 12 completion was highest in major cities, reaching 91% in South Australia and 85% in Victoria. Queensland has the smallest drop outside the city (79% to 72%), while New South Wales had the largest drop from city to inner regional areas (75% to 59%).

New South Wales remote areas have higher completion rates (64%) than inner and outer regional areas (both 59%). The largest gap is in the Northern Territory, where completion falls from 64% in outer regional areas to just 19% in remote areas.

Socio-economic background

Parents' education and neighbourhood socio-economic status both shape educational outcomes.

Parental Education

Comparing average NAPLAN outcomes by parental education

In every test and year level, students whose parents have a bachelor degree score highest on average, and students whose parents completed Year 11 or below score lowest. Grammar and Punctuation and Numeracy show the widest spread of results, while Spelling shows the narrowest spread and the smallest gap between education levels.


There is a general trend that higher scoring NAPLAN students also have higher parent education levels.

Year 12 Completion by Socioeconomic group (%)

ABS Index of Relative Socio-Economic Disadvantage was used to calculate socio-economic status (SES) on the basis of students' home address postcode.
Low SES is the average of the lowest 3 deciles, medium SES is the average of the middle 4 deciles and high SES is the average of the top 3 deciles.


South Australia has the highest Year 12 completion across all socio-economic levels compared to other states. Students from the Low socio-economic neighbourhoods in Northern Territory have the lowest completion proportion, with less that 1 in 4 students in these neighbourhoods completing their secondary education.

Socioeconomic circumstances and geographic location can influence how students participate in education, complete their studies and achieve their academic potential.


Where students live and their parents education are both linked to how they do at school. Completion rates differ between states and fall with remoteness, and NAPLAN scores rise with parents education in every test. These patterns show that opportunity is not evenly spread across Australia.
